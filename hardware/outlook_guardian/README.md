# Outlook Guardian board

A KiCad 10 project for a single board that replaces the breadboard build described in
[the hardware guide](../../docs/pages/outlook_guardian/hardware.md).

**Status: revision 0.1, not yet built.** The schematic passes ERC and the board passes DRC, but no
board has been made and no engineer has reviewed the layout. Read [Before ordering](#before-ordering)
first.

## Form factor

The board follows the [Hat Labs HALMET](https://github.com/hatlabs/HALMET-hardware) (CC BY 4.0), so it
fits the same 100 x 68 x 50 mm enclosure. These are at HALMET's exact positions:

- the outline (89 x 58 mm, concave corners, tab on the right) and the two M3 slots,
- the module, with its antenna overhanging the board edge,
- the 2x10 GPIO header and the 1x4 display socket, with the same power and ground pins,
- the NMEA 2000 terminal block (GND, V+, CAN-H, CAN-L), the USB socket, the BOOT and RESET buttons
  and the EN/IO0 header.

HALMET uses an ESP32; this board uses the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 the firmware needs. The module
outline is the same, but the GPIO numbers are not, so the signal pins of the headers carry ESP32-S3
pins.

## Pins

The on-board functions use the pins the firmware already defaults to, so no build flag changes.

| Function | GPIO |
|----------|------|
| Amplifier LRC, BCLK, DIN | 4, 5, 6 |
| Display SDA, SCL | 8, 9 |
| Acknowledge, Snooze buttons | 15, 16 |
| CAN TX, RX | 17, 18 |
| USB D-, D+ | 19, 20 |
| RGB LED (SK6812) | 48 |
| BOOT button | 0 |

GPIO header (J3), pin 1 is the square pad:

| Pin | Signal | Pin | Signal |
|-----|--------|-----|--------|
| 1 | 3V3 | 2 | IO21 |
| 3 | GND | 4 | IO1 |
| 5 | IO11 | 6 | IO2 |
| 7 | IO12 | 8 | IO7 |
| 9 | IO13 | 10 | IO10 |
| 11 | IO14 | 12 | GND |
| 13 | IO41 (MTDI) | 14 | GND |
| 15 | IO40 (MTDO) | 16 | GND |
| 17 | IO39 (MTCK) | 18 | GND |
| 19 | IO42 (MTMS) | 20 | 3V3 |

Display socket (J5): GND, 3V3, SCL, SDA, with GND next to the BOOT button. UART0 TX and RX, 5 V,
3.3 V and GND are on test pads on the back.

## Circuit

- **Supply, 9 to 32 V:** resettable fuse, TVS diode, reverse-polarity Schottky, input filter, then an
  XL1509-5.0 buck converter to 5 V (the converter HALMET uses, in its 5 V version).
- **3.3 V:** linear regulator from 5 V.
- **USB:** Micro-B on the ESP32-S3's native USB, with ESD protection. USB also powers the board
  through a fuse and a diode, so USB and the boat supply can be connected together.
- **Audio:** MAX98357A at 5 V, ferrite beads and capacitors on the speaker outputs.
- **NMEA 2000:** SN65HVD230 with a TVS diode, no termination resistor.
- **Buttons:** pull-up, series resistor, capacitor and ESD diode on each input.

| File | Content |

| `tools/` | The scripts that generated the project |

The board has four layers: signals and ground pour on the outer layers, a ground plane on inner
layer 1, and 3.3 V with a 5 V area on inner layer 2.

## Regenerating

The schematic and the board were generated by the scripts in `tools/` and can be edited in KiCad like
any other project. **Running the scripts again overwrites edits made in KiCad.**

```bash
tools/check.sh    # schematic from tools/design.py, then ERC
tools/route.sh    # board from the schematic and tools/placement.py, autoroute, DRC
tools/fab.sh      # fabrication files into fab/
```

`route.sh` needs Java and the [Freerouting](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting) 1.9.0 jar; set
`FREEROUTING` to its path. Later Freerouting versions did not terminate on this board.

## Before ordering

Checked by tools:

- ERC: one warning, the pin type of the amplifier's thermal pad in KiCad's own symbol.
- The netlist exported from the schematic matches `tools/design.py` on every pin.
- DRC: no violations, no unconnected items, no schematic mismatch, at 0.2 mm track and clearance,
  0.6/0.3 mm vias.

Not checked, and needing a person:

1. **Layout review.** The tracks come from an autorouter. Look at the buck converter loop (U1, D3,
   L2, C2 to C4), the speaker outputs and the USB pair, which is not routed as a differential pair.
2. **Antenna.** The USB socket is within the 15 mm clearance Espressif recommends around the antenna,
   as on HALMET. The module footprint's keep-out was narrowed to allow it.
3. **Part numbers.** They are in the `MPN` field. Ratings were checked against distributor listings
   for F1 and L2 only. None has a supplier order code, and none was checked for stock.
4. **F1 and D1 at 24 V.** F1 is rated 33 V and D1 is an SMBJ33CA, close to the 32 V top of the range.
   For a 24 V boat, choose a 60 V fuse and check the clamp voltage against U1's 40 V maximum.
5. **U2 output capacitor.** Confirm in the AZ1117C datasheet that it is stable with ceramic
   capacitors, or change C10.
6. **USB back-feed.** With USB only, 5 V reaches the output of U1 through L2. Confirm that the
   XL1509 tolerates this, or add a diode.
7. **Display clearance.** Check the height of the parts under the display module once its
   orientation on the socket is known.
8. **A first board is a prototype.** Bring it up on a bench supply with a current limit before
   connecting it to a boat.
